# Short-Side Research Findings v3 — Exhaustive Sweep + LONG Regime Filter + Combined Backtest

**Date:** 2026-04-26
**Window:** 2024-01-01 → 2026-04-24 (581 trading days, strict full-session-clean)
**Methodology:** Plan in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-04-26-short-side-research-program.md`. This run extends it to 134 short variants (BH-FDR q=0.10), 14 LONG regime kill-switch variants, and combined-portfolio IS/OOS at 2025-06-30 split.

---

## TL;DR

**Three independent results, in order of confidence:**

1. **The COMBINED strategy (LONG with regime kill-switch + SHORT mirror conditioned on the inverse regime) is a genuine, complementary improvement** that holds in OOS — but the improvement is modest (OOS PF 1.78 vs canonical 1.52, ~17% better on risk-adjusted basis), not the 30%+ that IS-only numbers suggested. Recommend **shadow-track in paper-forward**, not yet promote.

2. **No individual SHORT variant survived BH-FDR at q=0.10 across 134 tests** — but a coherent CLUSTER of regime-conditioned mirror shorts (5d_return < −1%, ATR-percentile > 1.3, 5d_return < −1.5%) consistently shows positive IS expectancy. Underpowered as individuals; meaningful as a family.

3. **The LONG regime kill-switch ALONE does NOT improve OOS performance** despite looking great IS — classic overfitting to historical drawdowns. The combined recipe works *only because* the SHORT picks up profit on the days the LONG halts. Filter alone = bad. Combined = good.

---

## What was tested

- **134 short-side variants** generated by combinatorial expansion across 4 setup triggers (mirror, naive_breakdown, failed_long_fade, vwap_rejection) × OR-class subsets × time windows × stop/RR × 12 regime filters
- **14 LONG-side regime kill-switches** (5d/10d/20d return thresholds, SMA slope, gap %, ATR percentile) applied to canonical Tier 1 and A+
- **12 combined portfolios** (4 LONG configs × 3 SHORT configs)
- **IS/OOS split** at 2025-06-30 for survivors and combined strategies

### What the OOS table says, plainly

1. **LONG filter alone overfits IS.** Tier 1 + halt-5d<−1% has IS PF 2.62 but OOS PF 1.36 — *worse than canonical OOS (1.52)*. The filter removes good days from the OOS sample. **Standalone LONG regime kill-switch is NOT a win.**

2. **Combined strategy DOES improve OOS.** Best Tier 1 combined OOS PF is 1.78 (vs canonical OOS 1.52). That's +17% on risk-adjusted basis. The improvement is real but modest — not the 30%+ that IS suggested.

3. **The combined improvement comes from the SHORT recovering profit on days LONG halted.** OOS Tier 1 LONG-filtered alone: $369. Combined (with short): $758. The short side adds $389 OOS, almost exactly compensating for the $303 OOS that the filter removed from canonical ($672 → $369).

4. **A+ OOS samples are too small to make strong claims** (n=13-24). The combined A+ strategies look good but variance is high.

5. **Drawdown improvement is robust.** Across all configs, OOS DD is ≤$259 (vs canonical $417). The combined strategy's risk-adjusted profile is genuinely better.

## What this means for executable strategy

### Honest reading of the evidence
- **Canonical Tier 1 LONG:** The conservative choice. Known OOS PF 1.52, DD $417. Sample n=89.
- **LONG Tier 1 with regime filter alone:** *Worse* OOS than canonical. Reject.
- **Combined Tier 1 LONG-filtered + SHORT regime:** OOS PF 1.78, DD $173-259. Better than canonical, but small sample at every step (OOS short n=11-15).
- **Combined A+:** Even better in point estimates but OOS samples too small for confidence.

### What I recommend — three honest options

#### Option 1 (most conservative — DO THIS): Shadow-track the combined strategy alongside canonical
- Keep `phase6-canonical-strict-2024plus` tag as the executable canonical (Tier 1 LONG only).
- Add the combined LONG-filtered + SHORT regime as **two new shadow rules** in the daily paper-forward journal:
  - `tier1_combo_5dret`: Tier 1 LONG with halt-5d<−1.5%, plus SHORT mirror_normal_5dret<−1.0% (s40/rr1.25)
  - `a_plus_combo`: A+ LONG with halt-5d<−0.5%, plus SHORT mirror_normal_atr>1.3 (s40/rr1.25)
- Wait until each combined strategy has 50+ forward trades. Then formally compare to canonical via promotion gate.

#### Option 2 (medium aggressiveness — DO THIS LATER, AFTER PHASE 6.5 RUNS FOR 1 MONTH): Run combined as the executable canonical
- Switch live execution to `tier1_combo_5dret` (PnL $2,857 IS, PF 2.03 IS / OOS PF 1.78).
- Justification: better risk-adjusted profile, smaller drawdown, OOS evidence positive.
- Risk: small OOS samples; may degrade further on truly forward data.

#### Option 3 (NOT recommended): Switch to LONG-filter-only
- IS looks better but OOS is *worse* than canonical. Don't do this.

## Honest statistical verdict

- **No individual SHORT variant clears BH-FDR.** If we hold ourselves to formal significance testing, the answer is "no edge."
- **The cluster of regime-conditioned shorts is structurally coherent.** Multiple variants point the same direction across multiple regime definitions and stop sizes. This is meaningful even if individuals fail FDR.
- **The combined strategy's OOS improvement is real but modest.** OOS PF improves 1.52 → 1.78 for Tier 1 combined, with similar or lower DD. Not transformative. Not zero.
- **All small samples.** Forward evidence is required before any strategy change.

---

## Mechanism story (why the combined works structurally)

The two pieces are mutually exclusive:
- **LONG halts** when 5-day MNQ return is weak (regime says "uptrend broken")
- **SHORT fires** when ATR-pct > 1.3 OR 5-day MNQ return is weak (regime says "vol-driven or weakness")

When the regime turns weak:
- Canonical LONG would still fire and lose / underperform
- Combined: LONG sits flat, SHORT picks up edge

When the regime is healthy:
- Canonical LONG fires normally
- Combined: same — SHORT regime not triggered, LONG operates as before

The improvement comes from *replacing low-quality LONG days with higher-quality SHORT days*, not from adding pure additional alpha.

This is a **dynamic side-selection strategy**, not a "two-strategy stack." That's a meaningful conceptual upgrade if it holds up in forward.

---

## What we should NOT do based on this research

1. **Promote combined to canonical without forward evidence.** OOS samples are 11-37 trades depending on rule. Real statistical confidence requires 50+ forward trades.
2. **Add the LONG regime filter alone.** It looks great IS but worse OOS than canonical.
3. **Continue searching for new short variants on this same data.** The Bonferroni / BH-FDR budget is spent. More tests just inflate false-discovery risk.
4. **Conclude shorts "work" based on this data alone.** They show suggestive but underpowered signal. Forward evidence is the only honest path forward.

---

## What we SHOULD do

1. **Update Phase 6.5 paper-forward to include the combined-strategy shadow rules** (in addition to existing Tier 1 + A+ canonicals + Tier 2 + A+ shadows).
2. **Set explicit promotion gates** for combined strategies: 50+ forward trades, OOS PF stays above 1.5 (Tier 1) / 2.5 (A+), no >30% degradation vs current OOS.
3. **Re-run this exhaustive sweep when live CME data lands** — the regime patterns may differ on tick-quality data.
4. **Close the broad-search question.** No further short-side hypothesis hunting on this dataset.
